# Package Deep-Dive

Part of [CompanyBlocker Architecture](../architecture.md): detail behind [§6](../architecture.md#6-package-deep-dive).

[The design principles doc](design-principles.md) covers why `packages/*` are external, standalone deliverables. `company_cleanse` is the leaf the others build on: `company_tokenize`, `company_vectorize` and `company_perturbation` each depend on it, and `company_classify` depends on `company_perturbation`, the one two-hop chain. `company_tokenize` and `company_vectorize` do not depend on each other. Tokenize reads cleansed names to train tokenizers and derive token statistics, which is what optimize then searches over; blocking gets its tokens either from `company_cleanse`'s packaged defaults or from a token set supplied per run, so the tokenizer reaches it as data rather than as an import. This section walks the packages in dependency order. `company_cleanse` and `company_vectorize` are covered briefly: their main material, the ISO 20275 grounding and the `ClusteringStrategy` contract with its DeepBlocker mapping, lives in [design principles](design-principles.md) and [DeepBlocker inspiration](deepblocker.md).

## `company_cleanse`

Deterministic company-name cleansing and company-type resolution, grounded in ISO 20275 (see [design principles](design-principles.md)), with incremental, data-verified coverage rather than a complete implementation of the standard. Leaf package, no dependency on any other package.

## `company_tokenize`

Owns tokenizer artifact conventions, WordPiece and SentencePiece training, DataFrame- and file-based tokenization, and TF-IDF-derived stop, noise and rare-token candidates from a prepared corpus. It does not own corpus construction, system balancing, or parquet-discovery policy: those stay in `src/acquisition`, which hands this package prepared `system_uri`/`name` parquet and nothing more. Leaf package, no internal dependencies.

[The tokenization design journey](tokenization-journey.md) covers this package's train-split-versus-validation-split selection bug and the open question of whether the gate thresholds correlate with real blocking and matching quality. The package-level facts it does not need:

- **WordPiece's compatibility invariants are fixed rather than tunable**: `continuing_subword_prefix = "##"` and `special_tokens = ["[UNK]"]` are held constant, because changing either breaks comparability across archived tokenizer artifacts.
- **SentencePiece's `byte_fallback=True` changes what `unk_rate` measures**: it trends structurally toward zero rather than reflecting a genuine miss rate, and imposes a hard vocab-size floor of ~268 slots below which training fails outright.
- **The optimize search grid is banded rather than log-uniform**, because vocab size was measured to stop improving monotonically past an elbow point, consistent with [the tokenization design journey](tokenization-journey.md)'s finding that 100K-vocab candidates do not reliably beat 25-40K ones.
- **The candidate archive keeps unpromoted tokenizers so they stay comparable**: naive, prior-promoted and promoted candidates are each archived with an `index.json` and a `promoted`, `naive`, `optimize_candidate` or `manual` tag. An optimizer's win is a win on its proxy metrics, and whether those track domain performance is unmeasured in either direction. Establishing whether the relationship exists is planned, by scoring a promoted tokenizer against the unoptimized and prior-operational ones on real blocking output, and that measurement requires those models to still exist. `optimize/models/` scratch directories are purged on any config change, so an unarchived candidate is gone.
- **Out of scope**: fixed-length or padded vectorization for neural consumers, left to the downstream package that needs it.

This package feeds `company_cleanse` (packaged noise-word defaults sourced from its TF-IDF analysis) and is consumed by `src/acquisition`, `src/validation` and `src/blocking`. It is to expose the trained tokenizer vocabulary so `company_classify` can drop its own TF-IDF vectorizer.

The country-versus-global comparison harness has swept `ie`, `gb`, `fr`, `gleif` and `offeneregister` on the WordPiece backend, with `ie`, `gb` and `fr` promoting new tokenizers and `gleif` and `offeneregister` having no eligible candidate and staying on the naive baseline, leaving `global` open. SentencePiece is further behind: `ie` is its only finalized scope, run as a smoke test of the optimize pipeline against that backend; `fr`'s sweep has run with its result unrecorded, and `gb`, `gleif`, `offeneregister` and `global` have not been attempted. The noise-floor numbers are IE-specific and not re-measured for the others.

## `company_vectorize`

Owns the pluggable `ClusteringStrategy` contract (`build_target_index`, `build_backend_index`, `score_source_chunk`) every blocking representation implements, the similarity backends (`sklearn`, `svd_rerank`, `kmeans`/`hdbscan` partitioning, `lsh`, S-BERT), and the DeepBlocker mapping table in [DeepBlocker inspiration](deepblocker.md). Depends only on `company_tokenize`. That table carries what exists and what does not, including the dense HNSW backend still to come.

The similarity metric behind every sparse representation is cosine similarity. `sklearn.feature_extraction.text.TfidfVectorizer` L2-normalizes its output vectors by default, so a dot product between two vectors equals their cosine similarity (`sparse_similarity.py`'s comment states this), and the `sklearn` and `svd_rerank` backends both fit `sklearn.neighbors.NearestNeighbors(metric="cosine")`, confirmed directly: `NearestNeighbors(metric="cosine", algorithm="auto")._fit_method == "brute"`, since cosine has no ball_tree or kd_tree support. Scoring is therefore exact brute force at every backend except the sub-linear ones.

Measured against real `gleif -> gb` production output: cosine similarity on these vectors tolerates a little asymmetry between two names, such as an extra qualifying clause or a share-class suffix, because enough shared distinctive tokens dominate the score. Recall collapses once a true match's names diverge by more than about two tokens: ~97% when both names have the same token count after cleansing, against ~2% when they differ by two or more. It does not address short-name and brand-name matching (`Cisco` against `Cisco Systems Inc`), which needs an explicit resolution mechanism.

## Short-name and divergent-name matching

The findings below set the design direction for the short-name pattern layers, particle normalization in cleanse and derived-key expansion in the target index, and are measured against validation's partitioned metrics: precision and recall over the cleansed-different residual, with the raw-identical and cleanse-absorbed buckets carried as counts rather than folded into the rate.

**A blended recall figure measures pipeline fidelity, not matching capability.** Of 93,617 true-positive matched pairs in the real `gleif -> gb` comparison output, **99.8% (93,412) have byte-identical `name_cleansed` strings** between source and target. Only **0.2%, 205 pairs**, involve textual variation the similarity metric has to discriminate. A blended recall of 95.4% therefore mostly measures whether an already-identical string survives acquisition and cleansing unmangled, while recall on the non-identity partition is roughly 205/683, about 30%. `compute_pair_truth_eval()` partitions precision and recall into identity and non-identity matches for this reason, and any figure quoted without that partitioning is dominated by trivial identity matches.

**Name divergence is at least three structurally distinct problems:**

1. **Acronym and initialism** (`International Business Machines -> IBM`). A rule cascade derives **42.1% to 73.1%** of these pairs, measured on real Wikidata same-language official-to-short-name pairs, entity-clustered via `system_uri` with cross-language pairing excluded. The cascade is exact and no-stopword initials, subsequence-of-initials, prefix truncation, and a compound acronym-of-type-prefix plus retained qualifier pattern such as `Schwimm-Startgemeinschaft Leipzig -> SSG Leipzig`. Coverage is language-dependent: **73.1%** (`fr`), **58.5%** (`en`), **42.1%** (`de`). The `de` end traces to compound-word structure a whitespace tokenizer cannot see, since `Deutsche Krebsregister -> DKR` needs `Krebsregister` split into `Krebs` and `register` first, making a decompounding pass the targeted fix. Coverage is not recall: what indexing these derived keys recovers against the real non-identity partition, and what it costs in precision, is not yet measured.
2. **Word-level prefix truncation** (`Cisco Systems Inc -> Cisco`, `Coca Cola Bottling HD -> Coca Cola`): drop a trailing run of generic or descriptive words, keep a leading distinctive phrase intact. Measured against 391,684 real GLEIF matched pairs: 2,481 pairs (0.6334%) are prefix or suffix divergent (`de` 1.1523%, `fr` 0.5541%, `ie` 0.4189%, `gb` 0.2273%), roughly 50 times the acronym rate and enough to clear the prevalence bar. `gb`, the main validation target, has the lowest rate of the four, so the win there may be smaller than the headline rate suggests.
3. **Unrelated brand or trading name** (`Norges Statsbaner AS -> Vy`, a 2019 rebrand). Not derivable from the legal name by any algorithm, rule-based or learned, since the information is not in the input. GLEIF's `trading` name-variant type is evidence for this category, carrying real alternate full names such as `FSDH SECURITIES LIMITED` rather than abbreviations.

**A second axis crosses that list: whether a rule can close the gap.** The categories say what relationship two names have, not whether the difference is fixable by cleansing, which is the question deciding where work goes. Only the first of these three belongs to `company_cleanse`:

- **Convention.** The same name in a different house style: `O'Hara` against `OHara`, `Mc Hugh` against `McHugh`, a legal-suffix spelling variant, differing punctuation or word order. Finite and enumerable, so a rule closes it and the pair moves into the cleanse-absorbed bucket. Across 817,761 Irish canonical rows, 9,061 names carry the `O` particle and 9,260 carry `Mc`. Of those, 783 (8.6%) write `O` with a following space against 8,278 (91.4%) joined, and 1,553 (16.8%) write `Mc` spaced against 7,707 (83.2%) joined, for 2,336 spaced names in all. That is prevalence, not recovery: how many matches normalizing the particle actually recovers, and what it costs in precision, is unmeasured. A particle-normalization experiment in cleanse settles it, and it has to be scored on the affected subset alone, since at these prevalences a real delta is diluted 20 to 100 times in a whole-corpus blended metric. The approach accumulates rules of this size rather than seeking one large win. Where punctuation joins a particle to the rest of a name, collapsing it beats replacing it with a space, since the other source usually wrote the joined form and a substituted space invents a token boundary neither side had.
- **Corruption.** The same name, damaged: `Limiteda` for `Limited`, a transposition, a dropped character. Unbounded, so no rule enumerates it. This is what the residual holds and what fuzzy matching has to earn, and no category above covers it, since each of those describes an intact name in a different relationship.
- **Absent information.** Category 3: the input does not contain what would connect the two names, so neither a rule nor a learned model recovers it.

The test when a pattern surfaces in the residual is whether it is enumerable. If it is, it is a cleanse rule and the pair should stop appearing in the residual; if not, it stays and is a blocking problem. That gives two independent axes of control, where cleansing could absorb more and where blocking must compensate, and re-running under a different cleanse profile moves pairs between tiers and measures the first directly.

**Category 2 is a set-containment problem and a blocking-recall question.** Whether "Coca Cola" and "Coca Cola Bottling Inc" are the same legal entity is a downstream matching decision; for candidate generation the question is only whether they land in the same block. Every token of the shorter name is typically present unchanged inside the longer one. Cosine similarity, over TF-IDF or a pooled dense embedding alike, normalizes by both vectors' size, so adding tokens to one side lowers the score even though the shared content did not change, and swapping representations does not fix it, since pooling a dense embedding is still a symmetric aggregation with the same dilution. The targeted fix is stripping low-salience tokens from both names before comparing. `company_tokenize`'s TF-IDF noise-word derivation already produces a continuous per-token document-frequency and IDF score while building its noise-word profiles, which identifies generic tokens with no new statistical fitting. That data is per-language: a word generic in one language's company names need not be generic in another's, so a consumer selects by the record's own language rather than applying one list universally.

**The alias and name-variant rows GLEIF and Wikidata already provide are also self-ground-truth for validating candidate representations**, distinct from using them to generate training pairs. Every name variant recorded under one `system_uri` is asserted by the data model to be the same entity, with no cross-system join needed. That allows checking whether a candidate representation clusters an entity's own recorded variants together, over a much larger population than the cross-system `gleif -> gb` matched ground truth. It needs the same stratification discipline as the acronym measurement: a variant pair with no shared signal, the unrelated-brand category, is not solvable by any string-based representation, and blending it into a collapse rate makes a working representation look worse than it is. It is also not a representative sample of the target population, since well-known entities carry richer alias data than obscure ones, so it is read as an orthogonal signal alongside the cross-system numbers rather than a replacement.

**The design is a union of independently-named heuristic layers at candidate-generation time, not a single unified distance function or agglomerative clustering.** Agglomerative clustering needs one coherent similarity function, and the obvious candidate fails at ~2% recall on divergent names; building a better unified function requires already knowing which of the three categories applies. Each layer, canonical name, the known name-variant keys, and the derived acronym and significant-token keys, stays separate, named and independently measurable, with each recovering 1-5% of the residual and several stacking, the shape the acronym cascade itself showed in reaching 73%, 58% and 42% through four separate rules with none dominant. This is additive to the existing blocking design bar, set by the partition backends, the short-name stem and the declined initialism signal: candidate generation tolerates false positives, since a spurious extra key costs one downstream comparison rather than correctness, so a union maximizes recall recovery at that stage. An agreement or confidence signal across layers belongs at the pruning and scoring stage as a precision refinement, deferred until there is a measured need.

**Ground truth has a separate gap.** `match_ops.py`'s `company_number_key` join, a single normalization pass stripping non-alphanumerics and uppercasing, misses genuine matches for reasons such as registration-authority prefixes present in one system's `company_number` and not another's. The fixable part is closed, independently of and without blocking the matching work above, since it affects only which `(source, target)` pairs get a `match_uri` label injected into the evaluation set, not the matching mechanism being evaluated. It added leading-zero tolerance for all jurisdictions, a Germany-specific trailing court-register suffix strip, and a France-specific SIRET-to-SIREN reduction, recovering +2,430 real `match_uri` matches across `gleif -> {gb, ie, fr, offeneregister}` (`de` +2,408, `fr` +13, `ie` +9, `gb` 0). GB was surveyed and has no fixable pattern, so no GB rule was added.

## `company_classify`

Company-name validity and pair classifiers, with their training, evaluation and inference, and the package where [the DeepBlocker-influenced encoder track](deepblocker.md) lands. Depends on `company_vectorize`.

**What exists** is a supervised baseline: `TfidfLogRegClassifier` and `TfidfMlpClassifier` (both framed in the README as baselines, "not yet a transformer-grade production model"), `split_examples()` for a deterministic train/validation/test split, `cross_train()` to fit multiple models on one shared split, standard binary metrics (precision, recall, F1, PR-AUC), and batch inference. There is no class-imbalance reweighting or score calibration: the README states that threshold recommendations need system-specific holdout validation before being trusted. The baseline and its blocking-oriented metrics are built; the threshold policy is not.

The three interchangeable synthetic, real-alias and short-name pair producers, reusing `company_perturbation`'s operators, and the pair-generation contract and entity-level split behind them have landed. So have a TF-IDF pair rescorer, modelled on DeepBlocker's CTT but with no trained branch and no extractable embedding, kept as a baseline with its ceiling on real name pairs measured, and a pooled-subword contrastive encoder trained over this repo's own tokenizers, whose pooled vector is an indexable embedding. The promotion report combining classifier and blocking metrics is not this package's: it belongs to analysis, and this package supplies its metric bundle. **The rest of the DeepBlocker-influenced track is [detailed separately](deepblocker.md)**: the trainable Siamese branch with a swappable architecture that CTT, CTT-cosine and Hybrid are built on, fine-tuning the pretrained sentence encoder, indexing the encoder's embeddings, the two static word-vector ingredients, the Autoencoder, Seq2seq and Trans-encoder trainers, and the evaluation harness and comparison protocol. Most of the track remains unbuilt, so this is the least-built of the five packages ([§1](../architecture.md#1-executive-summary)). Sharing the tokenizer vocabulary closes the loop back to `company_tokenize`: once it is exposed, this package's baselines can drop their independent char-n-gram TF-IDF vectorizer and reuse the trained one and its noise-trimming policy.

## `company_perturbation`

Deterministic name-mutation operators plus the profile and generation layer driving them: the engine behind `company_classify`'s self-supervised pair generation and the robustness-evaluation track (see [§10](../architecture.md#10-current-state)). Depends only on `company_cleanse`, for ASCII-homoglyph data, ISO-20275-backed company-type data and corpus-derived noise-word data. It has no dependency on this repository's dataframes, `system_uri` or truth model, so both the operators and the layer above them are independently publishable: a standalone install can load a profile from a directory it is handed and generate a perturbed corpus end to end.

**An operator is two functions, and the runner owns selection**: `sites(name, country)` declares every span in a name where the operator could act, and `change(name, site, rng, country)` makes one change at one of those spans. How many changes to attempt is the runner's decision, so one implementation of selection serves every family. A chain step asks for `attempts` independent trials each firing with `probability`, both absolute counts capped at the number of viable sites a name offers, so the damage a step can do is bounded by the name's own structure and not by its length. Seven families are registered (`SitedOperatorRegistry`, `sited_registry`), 13 operators in total: word-order shuffle; five typo variants (keyboard substitution, transposition, insertion, deletion, duplication); two diacritic and punctuation operators; two legal-suffix operators; phonetic (Soundex) swap; homoglyph transliteration; low-salience-word drop.

**`TAXONOMY.md` scored seven candidate families against four criteria** (in-repo data availability, relevance to current Latin-script systems, dependency cost, determinism compatibility) before anything was built. Two were **deferred with a stated revisit trigger**: full script-variant name rendering, blocked on a non-Latin-script name corpus entering the repository, with Wikidata flagged as the plausible source; and person-name nickname substitution, out of domain since no person-name data exists here. Company-word abbreviation substitution is deferred pending a check of whether Wikidata's alias sidecar contains usable abbreviation pairs.

**The legal-suffix operator ships its third design, the first two having been rejected in review.** Pooling all ~41 jurisdictions' ISO 20275 data produced nonsense, letting a UK "Ltd" substitute to an unrelated Australian classification label. Scoping substitution to one country still mixed unrelated regulatory tracks, letting GB "Ltd" become "CIO", a charity-specific legal form rather than a plausible variant of a private limited company. The shipped version uses same-canonical spelling variants plus a small hand-curated confusable-cluster table (GB and IE `{Ltd, Plc}`, DE `{GmbH, UG}`), the one curated rather than data-driven piece of this package.

**Non-obvious behaviour**: every trial draws exactly twice from the generator whether or not it fires, so a step that lands no changes and one that lands several advance the stream by the same amount. That fixed draw count is what lets a single generator run a whole chain, and a family drawing a variable number of values internally would change the outcome of every step after it. Separately, a chain whose order lets an earlier step destroy what a later one must recognise raises nothing: the site finder returns no sites, and `ChainResult.steps_that_landed_nothing` is what surfaces it.

Seven operator families are built, and the profile and generation layer consuming them lives in this package alongside them: `PerturbationProfile`, `parse_profile()`, `read_profile()`, `perturb()` and `perturb_records()` have no more dependency on `src/validation` than the operators do. Seeding is a plain `random.Random(seed)`, this repository's scalar-Python convention (see [design principles](design-principles.md)); a corpus walk gives each record its own generator, derived from the run's seed and the record's own id, so a row is reproducible independently of the order records were processed in and of how the corpus was sliced. A profile may name a `default_seed`, the sample a caller gets when it chooses none; the package never reads that field, and this repository resolves it in `validation.perturbation_profiles.resolve_seed`.

### Materializing perturbed datasets (`src/validation`)

`src/validation` turns a profile's generated output into an on-disk dataset that reads like any other system: same cleanse pipeline, same loader, same validation CLI. This is the piece that needs the repo's own dataframes and `system_uri` model, so it stays out of `company_perturbation`:

- A dataset is identified by the source system, the profile, that profile's version and the seed together, since one profile over one source under two seeds is two datasets of the same size. It lives under `artifacts/perturbed/data/<source_system>/<profile_id>/<version>/<seed>/` and is addressed by the validation CLI as `perturbed://<system>/<profile>/<version>/<seed>` with the same flags as any real system (`--sources`, `--targets`).
- Each row carries three identifiers: a `system_uri` unique to this perturbed variant, a `source_uri` pointing back to the record it derives from, and a `match_uri` carrying forward any cross-system ground truth the original record had, untouched.
- Malformed identifiers and identity collisions hard-fail rather than corrupting output, and a profile can be built up from multiple source systems without them colliding.
- A per-scenario tally of how many records each scenario drew, split by whether the name changed, is written beside the dataset and its path recorded in the run manifest.

Scoring robustness against this data, as opposed to producing it, is not built.
